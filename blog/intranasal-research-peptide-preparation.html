Intranasal Peptide Research: Overview

Intranasal administration is a delivery route studied in preclinical research for peptides where access to the central nervous system is part of the research question. The olfactory and trigeminal nerve networks provide an anatomical pathway in animal models that differs from systemic routes, which is why intranasal delivery appears frequently in research literature on nootropic and neuroprotective compounds.

In a laboratory context, preparing a compound for intranasal research involves dissolving the lyophilised peptide into a saline or bacteriostatic water solution and loading it into a spray device for use in the relevant research model. All steps described in this guide are for research preparation purposes only. These materials are not for human or animal consumption.

Preparation Steps

The following steps describe preparing a lyophilised research peptide as an intranasal spray preparation for laboratory use. All steps describe preparing research material — not guidance for human or animal use.

  1. Allow the peptide vial to reach room temperature. Remove the vial from the freezer or refrigerator and leave it for 15–20 minutes before opening. This prevents condensation forming inside the vial and the powder clumping.
  2. Wipe the vial stopper. Use an alcohol wipe on the rubber stopper of the peptide vial and allow 10 seconds to air dry.
  3. Draw up the saline. Using the transfer syringe, draw the required volume of 0.9% sterile saline. The volume of solvent added to the vial determines the concentration of the prepared research solution (see the concentration reference table below).
  4. Add the saline slowly to the peptide vial. Direct the solvent down the inside wall of the vial rather than straight onto the powder, to avoid foaming. Gently swirl rather than shake until the powder fully dissolves and the solution is clear.
  5. Transfer the solution to the spray bottle. Draw the prepared solution into the syringe using the fill adaptor and fill the nasal spray bottle. Label the bottle with the compound name, concentration and preparation date.
  6. Store correctly. Refrigerate the prepared spray at 2–8°C away from direct light. Keep the cap in place between uses and discard after the appropriate shelf-life window.

Saline vs Bacteriostatic Water for Intranasal Preparations

Both sterile saline and bacteriostatic water are used to reconstitute peptides for research preparations. Each has different characteristics relevant to laboratory workflows:

SolventPreservativeStability windowNotes
0.9% Sterile saline None ~24 hours Included in the intranasal research kit. Suitable for single-session preparations.
Bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol) Benzyl alcohol 0.9% Up to 28 days Extends the usable research window. Available separately from North Peptides UK.

For research preparations that will be drawn from across multiple sessions, bacteriostatic water is the standard choice, as the benzyl alcohol suppresses microbial contamination in the prepared solution.

Storage and Handling of Prepared Intranasal Research Preparations

  • Refrigerate at 2–8°C between research sessions. Store the spray bottle upright and away from direct light.
  • Do not freeze a prepared solution — freezing damages the peptide structure. Keep the compound as lyophilised powder for long-term storage and prepare only the volume required for the current research session.
  • Label the spray bottle with the compound name, concentration and preparation date. If both Semax and Selank are in use simultaneously, clear labelling is essential to prevent mix-ups.
  • Shelf life: Discard after 28 days (bacteriostatic water preparation) or after approximately 24 hours (saline preparation).

For general guidance on storing peptides in both lyophilised and reconstituted forms, see the peptide storage guide. For reconstitution technique, see the reconstitution guide.
