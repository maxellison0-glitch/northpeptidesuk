What Is GHK-Cu?
A Copper Peptide Research Overview

North Peptides UK · 3 min read · Research Use Only

GHK-Cu is a copper-containing research peptide that is widely studied and frequently requested. This overview explains what it is, why it is studied, and how it is supplied and handled in the laboratory — strictly as a research compound, not for human or animal use.

What Is GHK-Cu?

GHK-Cu is the copper complex of GHK, a naturally occurring tripeptide (glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine) that binds copper ions. It is supplied synthetically as a lyophilised powder. Its solutions carry a characteristic faint blue-green tint, which comes from the bound copper and is completely normal.

Why Is GHK-Cu Studied?

GHK-Cu is a frequent subject of laboratory research. Scientific interest centres on copper-peptide chemistry and on skin and connective-tissue research models, where GHK and its copper complex are investigated in vitro and in cell-based studies.

This is an active research area rather than settled science, so GHK-Cu is supplied purely as a reference research compound. North Peptides UK makes no claims about effects in humans, and nothing here is guidance for human or animal use.

Handling and Reconstitution

GHK-Cu dissolves well in bacteriostatic water. Expect the slight blue-green tint from the copper once reconstituted — this is normal and not a sign of contamination. Add the solvent down the side of the vial, swirl gently, and refrigerate the prepared solution. Our reconstitution guide covers the full procedure.

Storage and Stability

In lyophilised form, GHK-Cu is stable and kept frozen for longer-term storage, away from light. Once reconstituted, refrigerate at 2–8°C, keep out of direct light, and avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles. With bacteriostatic water, a reconstituted solution typically stays stable for up to 28 days when refrigerated.
