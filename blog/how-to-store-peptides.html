How to Store Research Peptides
A UK Researcher Guide

North Peptides UK · 5 min read · Research Use Only

Correct storage is the single most important factor in maintaining research peptide integrity between preparation and use in the laboratory. This guide covers the storage conditions for both lyophilised (freeze-dried) and reconstituted research peptides — temperature, light exposure, freeze-thaw risks, and what to avoid — written for UK researchers handling these materials.

Why Storage Conditions Matter

Research peptides are relatively small, biologically active molecules. Incorrect storage can break peptide bonds, cause oxidation, allow microbial contamination, or degrade the compound through ice-crystal formation. Any of these can compromise the research material before it is ever used, making results unreliable.

Peptides — particularly in lyophilised form — are more robust than many researchers expect. Stored correctly, they retain their integrity for months or years. The key is understanding the difference between storing the dry powder and storing the reconstituted solution, because the two have quite different requirements.

Storing Lyophilised (Freeze-Dried) Peptides

Lyophilised peptides are the most stable form of the compound. The freeze-drying process removes water — the main driver of molecular degradation — and leaves a stable powder that can be transported at ambient temperatures for short periods and stored frozen for the long term.

  • Long-term storage: −20°C. A standard laboratory or domestic freezer works well. Most research peptides are stable for twelve months or more at −20°C in sealed, intact vials.
  • Short-term storage (days to a few weeks before reconstitution): 2–8°C in a refrigerator is adequate for most compounds.
  • Light: Keep lyophilised vials away from direct light and UV. Store in the original vial with the cap on, ideally in the original packaging or a light-protected container.
  • Freeze-thaw cycles: For lyophilised powder, there is no risk from occasional freeze-thaw, provided the vial remains sealed and dry. Repeated temperature cycling can gradually stress the material; minimise unnecessary handling and avoid leaving vials at room temperature longer than needed.
  • Humidity: Keep vials sealed until ready to reconstitute. Lyophilised powder can absorb moisture from the air, which initiates degradation. Do not open the vial until solvent is ready to be added.

North Peptides UK dispatches all lyophilised vials stored frozen and sealed. On arrival, transfer them directly to the freezer or refrigerator, depending on when they will be used in the research workflow.

Storing Reconstituted Peptide Solutions

Once a peptide has been reconstituted, the storage requirements become more demanding. Adding solvent introduces water — and without a preservative, microbial contamination risk — that is absent from the dry powder.

  • Temperature: Refrigerate at 2–8°C. Do not freeze a reconstituted solution. Freezing causes ice-crystal formation that physically damages the peptide structure and can permanently degrade the research material.
  • Light: Keep the vial in its original box or wrapped in foil between uses. UV light degrades many peptide structures, particularly those with aromatic residues such as phenylalanine or tryptophan.
  • Shelf life: With bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol), a reconstituted solution remains stable for up to 28 days when refrigerated. With plain sterile water, stability is limited to approximately 24 hours.
  • Labelling: Always label each vial with the preparation date and compound name at the time of reconstitution. With multiple vials in use simultaneously, clear labelling is essential.
Why Bacteriostatic Water Matters Here

The 0.9% benzyl alcohol in bacteriostatic water suppresses microbial growth in the prepared solution, extending the usable research window to up to 28 days. Plain sterile water has no preservative and is only appropriate for same-session preparations.

Notes on Specific Research Compounds

Most research peptides stocked by North Peptides UK follow the same storage pattern described above. A few compound-specific notes:

  • GHK-Cu — the copper chelate gives reconstituted solutions a characteristic blue-green tint. This is normal and does not indicate degradation. Store at 2–8°C and protect from light as with any peptide solution.
  • Semax and Selank — both dissolve readily in saline or bacteriostatic water. Reconstituted solutions should be refrigerated and kept away from light. These compounds are typically prepared in saline for intranasal research model preparations.
  • NAD+ — particularly sensitive to light and heat due to the dinucleotide structure. Reconstituted NAD+ solutions are best used within a shorter window than the 28-day maximum; within two weeks is preferable. Keep rigorously protected from light.
  • KLOW Stack — a four-compound blend handled identically to any single-compound lyophilised vial. Once reconstituted, refrigerate and use within 28 days. Two millilitres of bacteriostatic water per vial is a practical preparation volume.
  • Lipidated metabolic compounds (Retatrutide, Tirzepatide) — the lipid moiety can separate from solution if a reconstituted vial is frozen. Keep reconstituted solutions refrigerated and avoid freeze-thaw.
  • Epitalon and Pinealon — small tetrapeptide and tripeptide respectively, both stable in lyophilised form at −20°C. Reconstituted solutions follow the standard 2–8°C, up-to-28-day storage guidance.

Common Storage Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving vials on the bench — even a few hours at room temperature shortens the usable window of a reconstituted solution. Return vials to the refrigerator promptly after use in the research workflow.
  • Storing in the refrigerator door — the temperature in a fridge door fluctuates every time the door opens. Peptide vials belong on an internal shelf where the temperature is more consistent.
  • Freezing a reconstituted solution — the most damaging error. Ice-crystal formation physically damages peptide chains. For long-term storage, always keep the compound in lyophilised form and reconstitute only what is required for the immediate research session.
  • Opening a lyophilised vial prematurely — even a brief exposure to humid air introduces moisture that can initiate degradation of the dry powder. Open the vial only when you are ready to add solvent.
  • No preparation-date label — without a label, it becomes impossible to reliably track a reconstituted solution's age. Label every vial at the moment of preparation.
  • Using plain sterile water for multi-session preparations — sterile water has no preservative. Without bacteriostatic water, a reconstituted solution degrades within approximately 24 hours and should not be used across multiple research sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do lyophilised research peptides stay stable?

At −20°C in sealed, light-protected vials, most lyophilised research peptides are stable for twelve months or more. Short-term refrigeration at 2–8°C (for days to a few weeks prior to reconstitution) is also acceptable for most compounds.

Can a reconstituted peptide solution be frozen for longer storage?

No. Freezing a reconstituted peptide solution causes ice-crystal formation that damages the peptide structure. For longer-term storage, keep the compound in lyophilised powder form and reconstitute only the volume required for the research session.

How long does a reconstituted peptide solution last with bacteriostatic water?

Reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and stored at 2–8°C away from light, most peptide solutions remain stable for up to 28 days. Preparations made with plain sterile water should be used within approximately 24 hours.

Can research peptides be used in humans?

No. All research peptides supplied by North Peptides UK are strictly for laboratory and scientific research and are not for human or animal consumption.
