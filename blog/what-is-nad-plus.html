What Is NAD+?
A Cellular Research Compound Overview

North Peptides UK · 3 min read · Research Use Only

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every living cell and a frequent subject of cellular research. This overview explains what it is, why it is studied, and how it is supplied and handled — strictly as a research compound, not for human or animal use.

What Is NAD+?

NAD+ stands for Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide, a coenzyme present in all living cells. It plays a central role in cellular energy metabolism, acting as an electron carrier in the reactions that cells use to release energy. It is supplied for research as a lyophilised powder.

Why Is NAD+ Studied?

NAD+ is one of the most studied molecules in cell biology. Research interest spans cellular energy metabolism, mitochondrial function, and cellular-aging research, where NAD+ levels and the enzymes that use NAD+ are investigated in vitro and in model systems.

Handling and Reconstitution

NAD+ dissolves in bacteriostatic water. Because the vial holds a larger quantity than most peptides, choose the solvent volume that gives the concentration your work requires — our reconstitution guide covers the concentration arithmetic. Storage and Stability

In lyophilised form, NAD+ is stable and kept frozen for longer-term storage, away from light. Once reconstituted, refrigerate at 2–8°C, keep out of direct light, and avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles. Prepare only the quantity you expect to use within the stable window.
