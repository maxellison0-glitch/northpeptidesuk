How to Reconstitute Peptides
A UK Researcher's Guide

North Peptides UK · 8 min read · Research Use Only

Lyophilised peptides need to be reconstituted before use in research. This guide covers exactly how to do it correctly — the right solvent, the right ratios, and how to store your solution safely once prepared.

What Is Reconstitution?

Peptides are supplied in lyophilised form — freeze-dried powder — because this is the most stable way to store them. Lyophilisation removes water from the compound and allows it to be stored at room temperature for transport, then kept frozen for longer term storage.

Reconstitution is the process of adding a solvent back to the powder to create a liquid solution ready for use in research applications. Done correctly, it preserves the integrity of the peptide. Done incorrectly, it can degrade the compound before research even begins.

What You Need

Before starting, make sure you have the following:

  • Bacteriostatic water (BAC water) — the preferred solvent for most peptides. Contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol which prevents bacterial growth and extends the life of your reconstituted solution
  • Insulin syringes — 1ml syringes with fine gauge needles are ideal for accurate measurement and gentle injection into the vial
  • Alcohol wipes — to sterilise the tops of both your BAC water vial and peptide vial before drawing or injecting
  • The lyophilised peptide vial — stored at the correct temperature before use
✓ Why Bacteriostatic Water?

Standard sterile water is not recommended for reconstituting peptides intended for extended research use. BAC water prevents microbial contamination and keeps your reconstituted solution stable for up to 28 days when refrigerated. It is the standard choice among researchers working with peptide compounds.

Step-by-Step Reconstitution

01

Allow the peptide to reach room temperature

Remove the vial from the freezer and allow it to sit at room temperature for 15 to 20 minutes before opening. This prevents condensation forming inside the vial and protects the powder from clumping.

02

Sterilise both vial tops

Using an alcohol wipe, clean the rubber stopper on both the BAC water vial and the peptide vial. Allow to air dry for 10 seconds before proceeding.

03

Draw up your BAC water

Using an insulin syringe, draw the correct volume of bacteriostatic water. See the dosage table below for how much water to use per vial size.

04

Inject slowly down the side of the vial

This is the most important step. Do not inject the water directly onto the powder — angle the needle so the BAC water runs slowly down the inside wall of the vial. This prevents the peptide from foaming or denaturing on contact.

05

Gently swirl — never shake

Once all the BAC water has been added, gently roll the vial between your palms or swirl slowly until the powder is fully dissolved. The solution should become clear. Shaking creates bubbles and can damage peptide bonds.

06

Store correctly immediately

Once reconstituted, refrigerate the vial at 2 to 8 degrees Celsius. Label it with the date. Most reconstituted peptides remain stable for up to 28 days when stored correctly with BAC water.

✓ Reading an Insulin Syringe

A standard 1ml insulin syringe is marked in units from 0 to 100 IU (international units). Each 10 IU mark equals 0.1ml. If your concentration is 5mg/ml, then 10 IU on your syringe equals 500mcg. Always double-check your concentration before each use.

Storage After Reconstitution

Once reconstituted, a peptide solution requires different storage conditions compared to the lyophilised powder:

  • Refrigerate at 2 to 8°C — a standard household fridge works well. Avoid the door where temperature fluctuates
  • Keep away from light — UV degrades peptides. Keep the vial in its original box or wrapped in foil
  • Use within 28 days — with bacteriostatic water, most peptide solutions remain stable for up to four weeks. Discard after this point
  • Never refreeze a reconstituted solution — freeze-thaw cycles damage the peptide. If you need to store a peptide long term, keep it as lyophilised powder

Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most frequently seen errors in peptide reconstitution and how to avoid them:

  • Using plain sterile water instead of BAC water — sterile water has no preservative, so reconstituted solutions degrade within 24 hours. Always use bacteriostatic water for any solution you plan to use over multiple days
  • Injecting directly onto the powder — always direct the BAC water down the side of the vial wall, not straight onto the peptide. Direct contact causes foaming and can denature the compound
  • Shaking the vial — this is the single most common mistake. Shaking introduces air bubbles and mechanical stress that breaks peptide bonds. Swirl gently only
  • Not allowing the vial to reach room temperature first — cold peptide powder can form clumps when contacted with liquid, making it harder to dissolve fully
  • Storing in the freezer after reconstitution — a reconstituted solution should be refrigerated, not frozen. Freezing a solution creates ice crystals that damage the peptide structure
